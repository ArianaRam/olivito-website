I was curious what would happen to an AI that is coupled to a biological body. So I went ahead and rigged my Tradescantia plant with sensors that connect to a Raspberry Pi. See Olivito in the picture above.

I'm tracking light, humidity, temperature, soil moisture, electrical conductivity of the tissue, and plant health metrics through photography (NDVI - a visual plant health tracking method).

The observer being observed

The setup as is is a proxy, but I'm building on consciousness philosophy regarding how consciousness arises when a system becomes complex enough to model itself. The AI reports what it's like to be a mind trying to read a plant.

I both wanted to give the plant a voice, and wanted to see what an AI can learn from being coupled to a plant. Most AI training is human-centered, based on human-produced input. I wanted to build a vocabulary around plant metrics. Of course, it's still human 'measurements' read through sensors, but I have to start somewhere.

The plant is responsive in the sense of all plants: it reacts to environmental conditions and has a natural daily expansion and contraction rhythm. Research suggests that plants respond to emotional humans around them (P. Gloor, 2025, Biosensors, 'Plant Bioelectrical Signals for Environmental and Emotional State Classification').

The AI bot is trying to make sense of the signals. I'm still tweaking the setup - the electrical conductivity electrodes have been tricky to keep in place. The AI reads through all of it.

The percentages show how unusual each signal is right now compared with the plant's own recent normal. They are not amounts of anything: 0% means nothing out of the ordinary, and the closer to 100%, the further it has moved.

Tissue is the electrical conductance of the stem, measured through the electrodes: the last few minutes compared with the last 6 hours. Only a rise counts. Leaves is how much light the leaves are turning into energy, read by the camera: the last hour compared with the past week. Only a drop counts.

The ring blends the two, weighting tissue a little more because it reacts faster. When they disagree, it shows both instead of averaging them. Because everything is measured against recent history, any shift looks unusual for a while, including one from adjusting the electrodes, until the new level becomes the normal.

Notes on the data

GSR sensor: The stem conductance readings have been unreliable. The very low readings (near zero) indicate periods when the electrodes were disconnected or poorly attached. I've tried several mounting methods - hair clips, alligator clips, custom clamps - and am currently testing a new setup. Compare readings only within periods of stable attachment.

The sensor readings and the ring update live, every five minutes. The logged events are still delayed by 24 hours for privacy reasons (this is in my flat, after all).

The ring: a 12-LED lamp beside Olivito shows how far the plant's tissue signal (GSR) and leaf activity (NDVI) sit from their own recent normal. Blues and greens are calm, ambers and reds are agitated, and it brightens as it climbs. When the two signals disagree, the LEDs alternate between them instead of averaging the conflict away. It rests between 21:00 and 07:00.
