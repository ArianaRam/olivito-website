Plantki

Listening between human, plant and machine.

Ari Ramos

Plantki, a first film (2 min). Installation scenes are AI visualizations of a proposed work.

The question

What can happen when a human, a plant and an AI learn to listen to each other?

Plants produce tiny electrical changes as they live. A few early studies, including recent work with the same species I use, suggest these signals may shift with what happens around the plant, even with the state of a person nearby. The research is young. But it opens a question I can't put down: what is happening between us?

What exists now

Real prototype

It starts with Olivito, a Tradescantia rigged with home-built sensors and a Raspberry Pi. At home, Olivito's electrical signal already drives a ring of light: the colour you see is the plant's state, read live. You can follow the sensors on the live dashboard.

The installation

Proposed · AI visualization

A small, dark space where anyone can sit with living plants. Their signal becomes light, scattered and restless when the plants are agitated, gathering into one calm field as they settle. You don't need to know how to meditate. You can simply sit and watch.

Afterwards, visitors tell the system how they felt. No wires on anyone. Their experience becomes part of the data.

The form is deliberately simple, in the tradition of perceptual light spaces. The complexity lives underneath: a live biological signal, an AI translation, and a research question about what passes between the three.

Looking inside the AI

Research layer

I study the inside of language models, how they move from noise to an answer across their layers. In Plantki I want to use the same kind of tools to ask: what patterns did the plants teach the AI?

Why listen?

We usually listen to plants so we can grow them better for us. Plantki asks what happens if we listen to learn from them. Today it begins with one plant. Later, many plants could teach AI, not from what we say about nature, but from contact with living systems themselves.

Status: working prototype at home; the installation is being developed for an art-science residency. If you'd like to collaborate, host the installation or support the project, I'd love to hear from you: ramosariana@gmail.com
